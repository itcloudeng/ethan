Page 1 — Macromolecules
Before you read: What do you think a macromolecule is?
A large molecule.
Nutrition Facts
Fat: 8 g
Carbohydrates: 37 g
Protein: 3 g
What are the 4 basic types of macromolecules found in living things?
Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids
Page 2 — Monomers and Polymers
What are monomers and polymers and how do they relate to macromolecules?
Monomers are small building blocks. Polymers are long chains of monomers. Many macromolecules are polymers.
Easy memory: monomer = one building block; polymer = many building blocks joined together.
Page 3 — Carbohydrates
Structure / what are they made of?
Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in a 1:2:1 ratio.
Monomer: monosaccharide
Polymer: polysaccharide
Function
Fast source of energy for cells.
Examples
Grains, pasta, bread, candy, cookies.
Page 4 — Lipids
Structure / what are they made of?
Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.
Building blocks: fatty acids and glycerol
Functions
Long-term energy, insulation, and part of cell structure.
Examples
Fats, oils, waxes, cholesterol, triglycerides, phospholipids.
Coloring directions
Fatty acids: blue
Glycerol: orange
Phosphate group: red
Page 5 — Proteins
Structure / what are they made of?
Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen.
Monomer: amino acid
Amino acids are held together by peptide bonds.
Functions
Enzymes, structure and support, defense, and transport.
Examples
Meats, nuts, beans, enzymes.
Page 6 — Nucleic Acids + Vocabulary
Nucleic acid structure
Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and phosphorus (CHONP).
Monomer: nucleotide
Nucleotide parts: sugar, phosphate group, nitrogenous base
Function
Contain instructions to build proteins.
Examples
DNA and RNA

14. Describe 3 complications if the body does not take in enough protein.
Weaker body support, weaker defense, and slower chemical reactions.
Note: this is an inference from the protein functions listed in the packet: structure/support, defense, and enzymes that help chemical reactions.
15. How are carbohydrates and lipids similar?
Both contain carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen and can provide energy.
How are they different?
Carbohydrates provide fast energy. Lipids provide long-term energy and also help with insulation and cell structure.
