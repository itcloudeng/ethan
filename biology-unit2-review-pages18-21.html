Page 18 — I. Chemical Bonding
1. Charges of protons, neutrons, and electrons
Protons: positive (+)
Neutrons: neutral (0)
Electrons: negative (−)
2. Changing the number of neutrons gives you an ______.
Isotope
3. Changing the number of electrons gives you an ______.
Ion
4. Atomic number is the same as the number of ______.
Protons
5. Label each bond type.
Electrons transferred: Ionic
Shared equally: Nonpolar covalent
Shared unequally: Polar covalent
Weak attractions between molecules: Hydrogen bonds
6. Difference between atoms and molecules
An atom is the smallest complete unit that makes up matter. A molecule is made of two or more atoms chemically bonded together.
7. How do you find the number of valence electrons?
Look at the element's group/column on the periodic table. For the main-group elements, the group tells you the number of outer-shell electrons.
8. What types of bonds give water its special properties?
Polar covalent bonds and hydrogen bonds. Water is polar because electrons are shared unequally, and water molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other.

Page 21 — Catalase Data
What temperature appears to be the optimum temperature for catalase?
32°C. Catalase produced the most product at 32°C: 36 mL.
How can you tell?
The reaction produced the greatest amount of product at 32°C, showing the highest enzyme activity.
What would happen if more catalase were added?
The reaction rate would increase if enough substrate is available, because more enzyme molecules could react with substrate.
What happens when temperature rises above the optimum?
The reaction rate decreases. High temperatures can change the enzyme's shape and denature it.
